# Marius's paid-ad creative direction

Distilled from the Summer Dream, Stadium and Recovery Walk iteration history. Current explicit direction wins. These are aesthetic preferences and known traps, not proof that a particular ad performs better.

## Collaboration preference

Marius clarified the desired workflow: **we create the strategy together, all meaningful details are known, then the agent produces to a high standard autonomously**. Do not interpret one-shot as inventing an entire unapproved strategy. Present the concept, shot/story map, genuine product proof, cues, music, ending, budget and quality criteria first. Lock them or record expressly delegated choices. After approval, make routine execution/refinement decisions without repeated requests; return for a real blocker or material change, not a better crop or a weak first generated take.

## What the viewer should feel and understand

Coachi is a coach in the athlete's ear, not a gallery of tracking dashboards. Build around one viewer problem and its observable benefit: stop pushing too hard; make recovery enjoyable; follow an interval workout without counting or thinking about the next step. Let the audience see the instruction, adaptation and progress. Show an attainable workout experience, not guaranteed fitness results.

Treat the ad as **a long activity compressed into a short story**. Intention/setup -> movement and challenge -> useful Coachi cue -> visible response -> continued journey -> earned finish. Omit unnecessary steps, but do not imply a false app flow or instant physiology. A branded ending cannot rescue twenty seconds with no understandable product purpose.

For a fresh20–25s concept, a useful starting shape is: opening/product0–3s; setup/action3–6s; challenge/cue6–11s; reaction/progression11–18s; payoff and brand18–24s. Adapt to the real cue durations and concept. This is an editing scaffold, not a universal clock, prescribed shot count or platform rule.

## Cinematic craft and coverage

- Reference films provide shot grammar: visual hook, action/details, route scale, camera movement, contrast and earned reveal. If a specific reference is available, watch it at normal speed and make a brief reference-beat -> new-Coachi-beat mapping before shots. If unavailable, say so and use accepted Coachi films; do not claim to have rewatched it.
- Use many purposeful short clips rather than a few long generic AI takes. Often0.7–2s works for energetic action; give UI reading, gestures, coaching response and ending more time. Cut on meaningful action/music accents, not a compulsory metronome.
- Alternate establishing/wide/drone, medium action, a small number of physical details and product POV. At least some shots must make the athlete genuinely small within the environment. A crop of the same close-up is not a new angle or drone shot.
- Let space breathe. Establish where the activity goes, show terrain changing coherently and use an expansive payoff. Steep uphill should look physically steep; exertion, footing and speed must match it.
- Keep travel direction, actor, gear, weather/wetness and route geography coherent. Avoid teleportation, repeated source handles posing as a longer journey, dead frozen tails, arbitrary speed ramps and gratuitous spin/flash transitions.
- Natural athlete build, posture, expression and subtle confidence. Avoid forced smiling, synthetic skin, isolated red nose/cheeks, morphing footwear/hands, earbud-neckband artifacts and stray belts behind the head. Inspect faces at native detail as well as phone viewing size.
- Keep props/animals secondary to the human benefit unless the new brief expressly makes them the subject. Preserve the supplied identity and anatomy; do not replace a real reference with a generic breed/look label.

## Product shots that feel real

The preferred view is the athlete looking down at a real-looking phone held in believable hands, with the surrounding track/path and suitable shoes/lower legs visible. The display needs to be large enough to read, but not so large that the physical setting disappears. Avoid a floating screen, detached studio mockup, tiny phone or plastic procedural hand.

Use native iOS content in a physical casing: a photographed/generated **blank** handset/hand plate plus deterministic local UI compositing works well; Blender is useful for handset depth, glass and perspective. Reuse the website three-phone renders as a material/readability reference when available, not as fake in-app evidence. Do not insist on fully3D fingers if the hybrid looks better.

Move subtly with body/camera rhythm. Hand, casing, screen and environment share the same coherent camera transform; the UI must not slide independently. Continue camera phase across screen changes, keep readable holds and avoid restarting the same zoom on each state. Roughly35%closer/0.28°roll were successful settings for one plate—not mandatory numbers for every phone.

Prefer recorded before/action/after interactions. For approved still-based demos, use a short understated touch indicator on the real control if that is the requested treatment; it shows editorial intent, not a recorded finger or successful OS action. A generated fingertip must actually contact the intended control before the screen changes, and cannot be called a genuine capture. Do not add click sounds by default.

Specific retained control lesson: on **“How hard do you want to train”**, touch **Continue**, not the intensity slider. Stadium's later Sets/Work/Rest white dials are separate controls; Recovery Walk does not acquire interval dials. Read the actual capture—new builds may change semantics.

Watch insert: athlete looks down naturally; do not push the wrist into the face/camera. Phone/watch need compatible UI state, rep, timer, BPM and pace semantics. If data are fictional examples, keep them clearly marked and do not present them as the actor's measured live session. Missing BPM is not permission to invent a connected sensor.

## Coaching and music

- Default to the actual **Performance Mode** voice, not the generic narration voice. Exact phrase ID/text is not enough; inspect the mode-specific file and listen to the take in context.
- Spoken English should be natural, clear and unhurried. Preserve complete words. Do not glue artificial gaps into “On target” or slow/stretch an approved take by default. If a new recording is required and authorized, prioritize a natural full phrase; audition internally before rendering the entire film.
- A correction needs a visible reaction beat before confirmation. An above-target cue is followed by easing off, then continued progress; “On target. Stay here.” should not arrive immediately or trigger harder running. A motivation cue is useful only if it adds something and does not crowd the correction.
- Interval work/rest changes follow the full relevant cue. Countdown clips may compress elapsed time, but must not look like a continuous30-second timer secretly running in four seconds. Let editing and route/workout progression signal the elapsed time.
- Music should feel like something people genuinely run or exercise to: strong groove, momentum, uplifting payoff, not generic corporate ambience. Recovery can remain positive and rhythmic without visually depicting high-intensity training. Pick for the concept; Summer Dream is a benchmark, not the mandatory track.
- Only music and clear coaching by default: mute generated/source footsteps, breathing, wind, rain, conversation, notification sounds and unrelated captured music. Do not add whooshes or clicks for polish.
- Music must carry **through the whole ad**, including the final branding, with a deliberate final tail/fade rather than premature silence or chopped beat. Duck smoothly around speech without removing the energy; judge the mix on phone speakers when available, not just a loudness number.
- For requested music comparison, provide3–4 genuinely different cleared exercise moods (for example uplifting melodic electronic, driving house, brighter dance-pop or relaxed rhythmic recovery). Keep picture, voice take, cue timing and output loudness comparable so the music is the variable. One selected default, working toggle/player, every export checked through its ending. Do not create variants by default when the user asked for one final cut or has already selected music.

## Branding and claims

Default: no sentence-sized editorial text across action footage. Use real app screens, a restrained early brand cue when the brief permits, understandable coaching and clear final identity. The accepted closing copy is **coachi** / **“Experience a coach that adapts”**—not the user's earlier spelling errors.

Integrate the ending into the last environment: camera follows the athlete's gaze or opens into suitable sky/negative space, with restrained typography, contrast and hold time. Stars on a black sky belong to an appropriate night concept; do not force a daylight story to jump to a detached star card. No arbitrary late shoe insert or unrelated screen before the payoff. Use official store artwork when applicable; availability badges and actual targeted platform must not be confused.

Latest explicit “no text” wins. A later request for ending branding is an exception for that ending, not permission to reintroduce paragraphs throughout. Required advertising disclosure must still be handled through a permitted ad/caption surface at launch; never hide material demo limitations to keep an image clean.

Do not turn the early brainstorm “professionals run70–80%inZone2,” a sub20goal or an invented HR-to-performance relationship into an unsourced product/physiology claim. Use verified product behavior and avoid guarantees. “10×better” means a demanding craft target, not a measured conversion promise.

## Examples, not mandatory plots

- **Summer Dream:** tunnel/headlamp -> real start-phone -> natural watch glance -> difficult outdoor running -> correction -> visibly adapted effort -> expansive mountain finish/sky brand. If repeating the lamp concept: true black until the lamp activates, beam from the forehead illuminating limited surfaces, no unexplained studio fill. An exposure fade cannot prove lamp causality. The old15s confirmation and0.7spause experiments are not global cadence rules; the later natural take was accepted.
- **Recovery Walk:** a woman recovering at easy walking effort, fit **female English Cocker** matching supplied references, freely enjoying nature as a background companion. This superseded the initial leashed/brisk/dog-hero direction. Share-photo/route and Performance-mode poster ideas were not permanent required shots; use only the current story/source approvals.
- **Stadium / Follow Along:** real intensity/work/rest setup -> repeat effort -> Done/recovery ->30/15/5/Start -> resume after the full Start -> open stadium finish. Benefit: just follow along. Later example watch BPM/pace were explicitly staged, not measured runner telemetry. The earlier sub20TargetRun brainstorm is a different concept.

Use descriptive names when revising: numbering changed and a nose correction was once applied to the wrong ad. If the user says everything else is perfect, lock all other film/audio decisions and fix only the named defect.
