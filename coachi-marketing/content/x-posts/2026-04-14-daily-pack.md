## Post 1
Type: `AI lesson`

Building with AI for runners keeps teaching me the same thing:

The hard part is not generating a smart sentence.
It is deciding what *not* to say when the runner is already overloaded.

If the watch shows 168 on an easy run, the model can invent 10 explanations.
The product has to decide which one actually helps.

## Post 2
Type: `shipping lesson`

One high heart-rate number cannot trigger the same coaching response every time.

Heat changes it.
Sleep changes it.
Stress changes it.
Hills change it.
Bad wrist HR changes it.

Shipping this product keeps forcing one lesson:
interpretation is harder than measurement.

## Post 3

Now the runner is solving a puzzle instead of just running.

That shift matters more than most running apps admit.
